Reviewer #5: (To be filled in by the Associate editor) - Metareview from the Associate Editor (minimum 60 words):
Notes to the Reviewer : This field is mandatory. Please put here your comments explaining your ratings of the paper and suggesting improvements

-The title and scope are not well aligned with the actual contribution. The manuscript is titled "Interpretable supervised feature extraction for time series and spatial data," but six of the seven benchmarks are temporal biomedical datasets, and the only "spatial" case, REMC, is essentially a one-dimensional genomic-position profile rather than a genuine 2D or general spatial dataset. The statement that the search is "agnostic" to temporal vs. spatial indexing is not sufficient evidence of a spatial-data contribution. The authors should either add a real spatial benchmark or revise the title and scope to accurately reflect that the method is primarily for multivariate 1D sequences. As written, the spatial claim overstates the paper's validated generality.

-The novelty relative to existing interval-based, shapelet-based, and ROCKET/MiniRocket methods is still not sharply defined. SublimeX appears to combine supervised interval selection, mean aggregation, Bayesian optimization, and a compact feature set. These ingredients are individually related to TSF/CIF/DrCIF, RDST, and MiniRocket. The manuscript needs a more precise technical argument explaining what is fundamentally new, not just that SublimeX produces fewer features. Table 1 is useful, but its interpretability ratings are partly subjective and do not by themselves establish novelty. The authors should clarify which design choice cannot be found in prior interval ensembles or shapelet methods and why that choice materially changes performance or interpretability.

-The objectives, method, and evaluation are not fully aligned. Objective (iii) claims to assess robustness, generalization, and interpretability, but interpretability is mainly demonstrated by traceability and SHAP analysis on only REMC and AZT1D. Traceability to a channel, transform, segment, and aggregation is a useful property, but it is not equivalent to clinical or biological interpretability. There is no expert user study, no formal fidelity metric, and no cross-dataset interpretability evaluation. The authors should either define a more operational interpretability metric and report it across datasets, or explicitly weaken the interpretability claim to "traceable feature descriptions" rather than broad interpretability.

-The internal validation design remains a major concern. The fixed 50/50 fit/selection split may stabilize candidate scores, but it also risks overfitting to a single internal split. The Resampling ablation is helpful, but it does not fully resolve the issue, especially for cross-subject tasks such as PAMAP2. The reported ~26% validation-test gap on PAMAP2 strongly suggests that the feature search captures subject-specific structure rather than subject-independent discriminative patterns. The authors acknowledge this, but they do not implement subject-independent validation during search. At minimum, the paper should discuss this limitation much more prominently, and ideally it should test a subject-held-out search protocol on PAMAP2 or another cross-subject benchmark.

-The evaluation protocol and leakage discussion need further clarification. The manuscript states that test folds, LOSO subjects, and AZT1D temporal test windows never enter segment search. This is important, but several details remain unclear. For AZT1D, segment discovery is performed once on pooled training windows from all patients; the authors should clarify whether this creates any cross-patient information sharing and how it affects per-patient generalization. For PAMAP2, the internal fit/selection split uses windows from the same training subjects, so the search-time validation is not subject-independent. The paper should more explicitly distinguish between avoiding test leakage and ensuring subject-independent model selection.

-The authors justify excluding HIVE-COTE 2.0, InceptionTime, and LSTM/GRU models on the grounds of interpretability and matched protocols, but this justification is not fully convincing. At least one strong black-box time-series baseline should be included to situate SublimeX's accuracy-interpretability trade-off. In addition, the statistical tests are welcome, but many reported differences are very small, such as AZT1D RMSE 30.98 vs. 30.76 and REMC AUC ties. The discussion should avoid implying broad performance superiority.

-The ablation study and reproducibility package still need tightening. Table 4 reports multi-fold ablations, but REMC uses only cell line E003, and the REMC baseline is not comparable to the pooled 56-cell-line result in Table 3. This inconsistency should be clearly separated or resolved. Several ablation variants, such as Optimized Aggregate and Pattern Search, do not consistently improve over the baseline, yet the paper sometimes draws strong conclusions from them. The authors should report effect sizes or confidence intervals where possible and avoid overinterpreting small differences.

-The writing, figures, and conclusions still require improvement. The manuscript has been reorganized, but several paragraphs remain dense, and some claims in the Abstract and Conclusion still sound stronger than the evidence supports. Dataset naming should be fully consistent, e.g., "MIT-BIH" rather than "MITBIH" or "MITBIIH," and typographical errors should be corrected. The SHAP analysis is only shown for two datasets, and the reasons for selecting REMC and AZT1D should be stated more explicitly. Figures and tables, especially SHAP and ablation figures, should be checked for font size and print readability. The Conclusion should be rewritten to match the actual contribution: a compact, traceable, competitive feature-extraction framework, not a uniformly superior or clinically validated solution.

Reviewer #12: This paper proposes SublimeX, a supervised interpretable feature extraction framework that iteratively discovers segment-mean features via Bayesian optimization, each defined by channel, transform, segment, and aggregation. The revision responds well to prior comments by adding objectives, related work, statistics, ablations, SHAP analyses, reproducibility materials, and limitations. However, the scope of spatial data in the title, the consistency between internal validation and external evaluation, external validation of interpretability, and comparison with strong baselines still need strengthening. Major revision is recommended.


Strengths:

1. The authors respond relatively systematically to the previous round of comments, which makes the method positioning and contributions clearer.
2. The feature design of SublimeX is attractive: compact, readable, and traceable to specific channels, transforms, and signal segments.
3. The experimental reporting is clearly strengthened: paired Wilcoxon tests, multi-fold ablations, per-patient AZT1D reporting, runtime, and baseline hyperparameters are added.
4. The interpretability demonstration has highlights: the SHAP analyses on REMC and AZT1D are consistent with domain knowledge, and the open-source and reproducibility materials are relatively complete.


Weaknesses:

1: The "spatial data" in the title still lacks sufficient support: REMC is essentially a one-dimensional spatially indexed sequence along genomic positions, not true 2D/3D spatial data, and no spatial benchmark experiment is included.
2. Internal validation and external evaluation are not fully consistent: PAMAP2 uses a same-subject 50/50 internal split, while external evaluation is LOSO, leading to a large validation-test gap; the Resampling ablation cannot fully replace subject-independent or nested validation.
3. Interpretability validation still mainly relies on traceability, fold stability, and SHAP on two datasets, lacking domain expert confirmation or evidence of interpretive consistency on more datasets.
4. Baseline coverage remains limited: strong time-series baselines such as HIVE-COTE 2.0, InceptionTime, and LSTM/GRU are not evaluated, and the exclusion justifications are insufficient for some methods, affecting the completeness of the "competitive" conclusion.


Questions:

1. Would the authors consider limiting spatial data in the title or abstract to "spatially indexed 1D sequences", or adding a true 2D/3D spatial data benchmark?
2. Can the authors adopt subject-independent internal validation for cross-subject tasks such as PAMAP2, or at least report the results of nested/repeated validation?
3. Can the authors add domain expert evaluation, or increase SHAP/stability analyses on more datasets, to more strongly support the "interpretable" claim?
4. Can the authors include at least one of HIVE-COTE 2.0 or InceptionTime, or provide a more detailed justification for why these strong baselines are not directly comparable?


Reviewer #14: Although the authors have provided extensive responses to the review comments, they have not convincingly resolved several key issues.
1. The response states that AZT1D RMSE is reported as "mean ± SD over those 23 per-patient errors (e.g. SublimeX 30.98 ± 5.65 vs. MiniRocket 30.76 ± 5.38)." However, the abstract still reports "31.11 vs. 31.18." Why does the abstract remain inconsistent with Table 3, despite the response claiming the abstract was revised?
2. The response claims that "the REMC baseline in Table 4 is not comparable to the pooled 56-line SublimeX score in Table 3." But Table 3 uses AUC for REMC, while Table 4 uses accuracy. How can readers assess the ablation conclusions when the metric itself differs between the two tables?
3. The response explicitly states "no leakage from test data into feature discovery." Yet it does not clarify whether each LOSO fold in PAMAP2 uses an independent Optuna study with a reset search history. If the TPE sampler retains knowledge across folds, earlier folds could influence later searches. How is this potential cross-fold leakage prevented?
4. The response explains the absence of SHAP analysis for five datasets by stating "There was no space to add SHAP on the remaining datasets." Given that the paper claims a general "interpretable feature extraction" framework, how can interpretability be demonstrated beyond the two selected datasets (REMC and AZT1D)?
5.The response says "Table 3 reports end-to-end wall-clock time." However, end-to-end time conflates offline feature search with online inference. Since deployment depends primarily on inference speed, could the authors report search time and inference time separately to allow a fair comparison?
6.All validation experiments in this paper are based on public benchmark datasets rather than real world clinical data. Has SublimeX been validated on real medical datasets? If not, how can its applicability in real clinical settings be justified?


Reviewer #16: The manuscript presents SublimeX, a supervised feature extraction framework that searches for compact and traceable interval-based features from different signal representations. The idea of combining iterative feature search with multiple signal transformations is interesting, and the experiments cover several biomedical datasets. I nevertheless have several comments that may help improve the presentation and positioning of the work:

1. There are still some inconsistencies in the section numbering. For instance, several subsections under Related Work appear to use the same numbering "1.2". Please check the section and subsection numbering throughout the manuscript and make the hierarchy consistent.

2. I noticed several unresolved figure references in the current manuscript. Expressions such as "Figure ??" still appear in Sections 3.2, 3.3, and 3.5. These cross-references should be carefully checked before publication, including references to figures, tables, and sections.

3. It is not always easy to follow how the data are processed and passed between the different components of SublimeX, particularly during iterative feature discovery and model evaluation. A concise pseudocode description or a more detailed workflow diagram would make the overall procedure easier to understand and reproduce.

4. Some dataset names and abbreviations are formatted inconsistently. For example, "MIT-BIH" is used in Section 2.5, whereas "MITBIH" appears in Table 2. Similar notation should be checked throughout the manuscript so that dataset names and abbreviations follow a consistent convention.

5. The discussion of spatiotemporal information is relatively limited, and the potential application scope of the proposed framework could be further discussed. In particular, its applicability to spatiotemporal prediction tasks deserves consideration, with recent studies such as TIDES (JSAC 2025) providing a relevant example.

6. Since the experiments involve different types of downstream tasks, the presentation of the results could be made easier to follow by distinguishing the task settings more clearly. For example, classification and regression experiments could be introduced separately, together with their corresponding evaluation metrics and results.

7. The current Methods section also contains material that is more closely related to experimental configuration, including the datasets, baseline methods, and evaluation metrics. It may be clearer to move these parts into a separate Experimental Setup section, so that the description of SublimeX itself is more clearly separated from the evaluation protocol.
