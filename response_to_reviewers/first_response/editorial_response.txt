Reviewer #1: (To be filled in by the Associate editor) - Metareview from the Associate Editor (minimum 60 words):
Notes to the Reviewer : This field is mandatory. Please put here your comments explaining your ratings of the paper and suggesting improvements

The manuscript "Interpretable supervised feature extraction for time series and spatial data", I would recommend major revision. The topic is relevant and the proposed SublimeX framework has potential, but several methodological, validation, and presentation issues need to be addressed before publication.

The manuscript presents an interesting interpretable feature extraction framework, but the novelty of SublimeX should be stated more clearly. The authors should explain how it differs substantially from existing interval-based, shapelet-based, ROCKET/MiniROCKET, and supervised feature extraction methods.
The title mentions both "time series and spatial data," but most of the paper focuses on time-series biomedical datasets. The spatial data contribution is not sufficiently demonstrated. The authors should either strengthen the spatial-data evaluation or revise the title and scope.
The abstract is informative, but it overstates competitiveness. SublimeX is not the best-performing method on several datasets, including MITBIH, Emotions, MIMIC-IV, PAMAP2, and SVD. The abstract should more cautiously describe the results.
The methodology needs more detail on the stopping criterion. The paper states that the loop stops when no candidate improves performance, but the exact definition of improvement, tolerance threshold, and validation metric direction should be clearly specified.
The use of a single deterministic 50/50 train/validation split during feature optimization may introduce instability and overfitting. The authors should justify this choice and discuss whether nested cross-validation or repeated validation would provide more reliable feature selection.
The internal feature-search validation split does not match the external evaluation design for some datasets, especially PAMAP2 with leave-one-subject-out validation. This likely explains the large validation-test gap. The authors should consider using subject-independent validation during feature search.
The manuscript should clarify whether feature extraction is performed independently within each training fold only. If any information from validation or test folds influences feature discovery, this would create data leakage.
The comparison with baselines is incomplete. Important state-of-the-art time-series methods such as HIVE-COTEv2, InceptionTime, and LSTM/GRU-based models are only mentioned as limitations but not evaluated. At minimum, the authors should justify why these were excluded.
The authors should provide statistical significance testing across methods. Many reported differences are very small, such as AZT1D RMSE 31.11 vs. 31.18 and REMC AUC 0.931 vs. 0.931, so claims of superior performance require statistical support.
The results show that SublimeX is competitive but not consistently superior. The discussion should better balance performance and interpretability rather than implying broad performance advantage.
The ablation study is based on a single fold only, which limits the reliability of the conclusions. The authors should either repeat ablations across multiple folds or clearly tone down the strength of the claims.
Runtime comparisons need clearer reporting. The manuscript reports runtimes for ablation variants, but does not compare computational cost against all baseline methods under the same hardware and settings.
The graphical abstract and Figure 1 contain small text and may be difficult to read. The authors should improve figure resolution, font size, and visual clarity.
Some figures, especially SHAP visualizations, are too small to interpret in the manuscript. Larger panels or supplementary high-resolution versions should be provided.
The paper should provide the exact hyperparameters used for LightGBM, CNN, MiniRocket, RDST, tsfresh, and catch22 to ensure reproducibility.
The authors state that SublimeX is open source, but the manuscript should include a permanent repository link, version number, and ideally a reproducibility package for all experiments.
The manuscript should better define what "interpretable" means in this context. Traceability to a signal segment is useful, but interpretability also depends on clinical or biological validity, stability, and expert confirmation.
The SHAP analysis is helpful, but it is only shown for two datasets. The authors should clarify why REMC and AZT1D were selected and whether similar interpretability patterns hold across other datasets.
The discussion should include a stronger limitations section. Important limitations include greedy search, local optima, risk of overfitting, high computational cost, limited baseline comparison, and reduced generalization in cross-subject settings.
Several writing and formatting issues need correction, including typographical errors such as "anaylsis," inconsistent capitalization of dataset names, and overly long paragraphs in the Discussion.
The manuscript would benefit from a clearer contribution statement at the end of the Introduction, listing the main methodological and empirical contributions.
The authors should avoid overstating clinical applicability. The datasets are benchmark datasets, and no prospective clinical validation or expert user study is presented.
The manuscript should clarify whether SublimeX is intended mainly for prediction, hypothesis generation, or clinical decision support, because the validation requirements differ for each use case.
The manuscript is promising and relevant to Pattern Recognition, but it requires major revision to improve methodological rigor, reproducibility, baseline comparison, and clarity of claims.


Reviewer #2: The manuscript presents an interesting and relevant approach to interpretable feature extraction for time series and spatial data, 
addressing an important gap between predictive performance and model transparency. 
The proposed framework is well motivated and demonstrates competitive results across multiple datasets, with a clear emphasis on interpretability. 
However, several aspects require improvement to strengthen the contribution. 
In particular, the objectives and novelty of the method should be more explicitly stated, and the distinction from existing approaches clarified. 
The experimental section would benefit from more rigorous statistical analysis, clearer reporting of reproducibility details, and stronger justification of evaluation protocols. Additionally, some claims regarding performance and interpretability should be moderated or better supported by quantitative evidence. The manuscript is generally well structured, but certain sections could be streamlined for clarity, and minor language editing is recommended. Overall, the work has merit, but revisions are needed to improve clarity, rigor, and positioning.
